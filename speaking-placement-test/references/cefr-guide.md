# CEFR guide for the report

Levels follow the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR), Council of Europe. The summaries below are our own words based on the "Structured overview of all CEFR scales". Do not quote the CEFR text in a report.

"+" marks the upper band of a level: clearly above it, not yet at the next one.

## Level names

- A1 to A2+: Basic user
- B1 to B2+: Independent user
- C1 to C2: Proficient user

Add ", upper band of B1" and the like when the level has a "+".

## Recommended level of study

The level being worked towards, not the one already shown.

- A confirmed upper band ("B1+") means the next level: study at B2.
- A plain level ("B1") means the same level: study at B1.
- A1 means study at A2.

## Five criteria for speaking, plus pronunciation

Judge each criterion on its own evidence. They rarely all sit at the same level.

**Range.** A2: basic sentence patterns and memorised phrases for simple everyday situations. B1: enough language to get by on familiar topics, with some hesitation and roundabout phrasing. Upper B1: explains the main points of an idea or a problem with reasonable precision. B2: clear descriptions and viewpoints on most general topics without obvious searching for words, some complex sentences. C1: a broad range, chooses how to say things without restricting the message.

**Accuracy.** A2: some simple structures right, basic mistakes systematic. B1: reasonably accurate with frequent patterns in predictable situations. Upper B1: generally good control in familiar contexts; errors occur but the meaning is clear. B2: relatively high control, no errors that cause misunderstanding, corrects most mistakes. C1: consistently accurate, errors are rare.

**Fluency.** A2: short utterances, evident pauses and false starts. B1: keeps going comprehensibly, though pausing to plan and repair is very evident in longer stretches. Upper B1: speaks with relative ease, keeps going without help. B2: fairly even tempo, few noticeably long pauses. C1: fluent and spontaneous, almost effortless.

**Interaction.** A2: answers questions and simple statements, rarely keeps a conversation going alone. B1: starts, keeps up and closes a simple conversation on familiar topics, repeats back to confirm understanding. B2: initiates, takes turns, ends when needed, helps the discussion along. C1: chooses suitable phrases to take or keep the floor and links to what others said.

**Coherence.** A2: links groups of words with "and", "but", "because". B1: links shorter elements into a connected, linear sequence of points. B2: a limited number of linking devices for clear, connected speech, some jumpiness in long turns. C1: clear, smoothly flowing, well-structured speech.

**Pronunciation.** A2: generally clear enough, listeners sometimes need repetition. B1: clearly intelligible, an accent sometimes evident, occasional mispronunciations. B2: clear, natural pronunciation and intonation. C1: varies intonation and stress to express shades of meaning.

## At work, by level

From the CEFR scales for meetings, co-operation, information exchange and presentations.

- **A2:** says what they think in a meeting when asked directly; simple, routine exchanges of information; a short rehearsed presentation on a familiar subject.
- **B1 and upper B1:** exchanges, checks and confirms facts in their own field; explains why something is a problem and discusses what to do next; compares alternatives; puts a point of view across clearly but finds debate difficult; gives a prepared, straightforward presentation and takes follow-up questions, sometimes asking for repetition.
- **B2:** takes an active part in routine and non-routine meetings; sustains an opinion, evaluates proposals, responds to "what if" questions; outlines a problem with its causes and consequences and weighs advantages and disadvantages; presents with reasons for and against and handles follow-up questions with ease.
- **C1:** argues a position convincingly and answers counter-arguments fluently; gives a clear, well-structured presentation of a complex subject and handles interruptions.

The line between upper B1 and B2: comparing alternatives is upper B1; weighing their advantages and disadvantages and justifying a choice is B2.

## Teacher's marks during the call (1 to 5)

Pronunciation: 1 often hard to understand, 2 understood with effort, 3 clear with a noticeable accent, 4 clear and easy to follow, 5 natural stress and rhythm.

Fluency: 1 single words and long pauses, 2 short phrases and frequent pauses, 3 keeps going but pauses to find words, 4 speaks at length with few pauses, 5 smooth and effortless.
