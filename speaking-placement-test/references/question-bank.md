# Question bank

Starter questions from UnifyHub's speaking test. Adapt them to your company before you use them.

Each question has a main question, a follow-up, a push (the harder follow-up that shows whether the person holds the level) and what to listen for.

## Skill map: the same skill at each level, in every profile

| Level | What the questions test |
|---|---|
| A2 | Everyday work facts: a normal working day, last week, plans. Present simple, past simple, "going to". |
| B1 | A story with a sequence (a problem solved), change over time, plans with reasons. Past tenses, present perfect, comparatives. |
| B2 | Justifying a choice or an opinion, hypotheses ("if you could change…"), explaining something to a non-expert. Contrast, conditionals, hedging, clear structure. |
| C1 | Trade-offs, speculation about the future, an abstract or sensitive topic (for example, an incident review for senior managers). Nuance, concession, register. |

The order of the slots inside a level does not matter. A role may replace one slot per level with a task of the same difficulty that the company asked for, as Engineering does (a status update at B1, an incident review at C1).

To write a role-specific set, keep this map and take the topics from the person's real work: their tools, meetings, clients and problems. Three examples follow: general, business and one role (engineering).

## General English

Everyday and general work topics. Use it when a company has no specific request.

### A2

- **Work and routine.** What do you do in your job? Describe a normal working day.
  - Follow-up: What do you do first in the morning?
  - Push: Which task takes most of your time, and why?
  - Listen for: present simple, time phrases
- **Past events.** What did you do last weekend?
  - Follow-up: Who were you with?
  - Push: Was it a typical weekend for you?
  - Listen for: past simple, regular and irregular verbs
- **Plans.** What are you going to do after work today?
  - Follow-up: And tomorrow?
  - Push: What would you do with a free day?
  - Listen for: going to, simple future forms

### B1

- **Solving a problem.** Tell me about a problem at work you solved recently. What did you do?
  - Follow-up: What happened next?
  - Push: What would you do differently now?
  - Listen for: past simple and past continuous, sequencing words
- **Change over time.** How has your job changed since you started?
  - Follow-up: What is the biggest change?
  - Push: Is the change good for your team?
  - Listen for: present perfect, comparatives
- **Plans and reasons.** What are your plans at work for the next year, and why?
  - Follow-up: What do you need to get there?
  - Push: What could stop you?
  - Listen for: future forms, giving reasons

### B2

- **Opinion.** Some people say remote work makes teams weaker. How far do you agree?
  - Follow-up: Can you give an example from your team?
  - Push: What would the other side say?
  - Listen for: opinion language, contrast, hedging
- **Hypothesis.** If you could change one process in your company, what would it be and why?
  - Follow-up: Who would be against it?
  - Push: What might go wrong?
  - Listen for: second conditional, cause and effect
- **Explaining to a non-expert.** Explain something complex from your work to a person from another department.
  - Follow-up: Why does it matter to them?
  - Push: What do people usually misunderstand?
  - Listen for: paraphrase, linking, clear structure

### C1

- **Trade-offs.** Is it better to be a specialist in one thing or to know a little about many things?
  - Follow-up: Which are you?
  - Push: What does the other kind of person do better?
  - Listen for: nuance, concession, precise vocabulary
- **Speculation.** How do you think your profession will look in ten years?
  - Follow-up: What makes you think so?
  - Push: What would have to be true for you to be wrong?
  - Listen for: speculation, complex sentences, idiomatic range
- **Abstract topic.** What makes feedback useful, and why is it so often badly given?
  - Follow-up: Is that a cultural question?
  - Push: How would you teach it?
  - Listen for: abstract argument, flexibility, register

## Business English

Meetings, emails, clients, negotiations and presentations. Use it when a company wants a business English check without a role focus.

### A2

- **Work and routine.** What do you do in your job? Describe a normal working day.
  - Follow-up: What do you do first in the morning?
  - Push: Which task takes most of your time, and why?
  - Listen for: present simple, time phrases
- **Past events.** What meetings or calls did you have last week?
  - Follow-up: Who was there?
  - Push: Was it a useful meeting? Why?
  - Listen for: past simple, regular and irregular verbs
- **Plans.** What are you going to do at work this week?
  - Follow-up: Who are you going to meet or write to?
  - Push: What do you need from other people to finish it?
  - Listen for: going to, simple future forms

### B1

- **Solving a problem.** Tell me about a time when something went wrong with a client, a partner or another team. What did you do?
  - Follow-up: What happened next?
  - Push: What would you do differently now?
  - Listen for: past simple and past continuous, sequencing words
- **Change over time.** How has the way your team communicates changed since you joined?
  - Follow-up: What is the biggest change?
  - Push: Is the change good for your team?
  - Listen for: present perfect, comparatives
- **Plans and reasons.** What do you want to achieve at work in the next year, and why?
  - Follow-up: What do you need to get there?
  - Push: What could stop you?
  - Listen for: future forms, giving reasons

### B2

- **Opinion.** Some people say most meetings could be an email. How far do you agree?
  - Follow-up: Can you give an example from your work?
  - Push: What would the other side say?
  - Listen for: opinion language, contrast, hedging
- **Hypothesis.** If an important client asked for something your company could not deliver on time, how would you handle the conversation?
  - Follow-up: What would you offer instead?
  - Push: What might go wrong?
  - Listen for: second conditional, cause and effect
- **Explaining to a non-expert.** Explain what your company does, and how it earns money, to someone who has never heard of it.
  - Follow-up: Why would a client choose you?
  - Push: What do people usually misunderstand?
  - Listen for: paraphrase, linking, clear structure

### C1

- **Trade-offs.** In a negotiation, when is it right to give something up now to keep the relationship for the future?
  - Follow-up: Where is the limit for you?
  - Push: Argue the opposite view.
  - Listen for: nuance, concession, precise vocabulary
- **Speculation.** How do you think business communication will change in the next ten years?
  - Follow-up: What makes you think so?
  - Push: What would have to be true for you to be wrong?
  - Listen for: speculation, complex sentences, idiomatic range
- **Abstract topic.** What makes a person persuasive in business, and can it be learned?
  - Follow-up: Is that a cultural question?
  - Push: How would you teach it?
  - Listen for: abstract argument, flexibility, register

## Engineering

Developers, DevOps and technical leads: updates, blockers, incidents, APIs and integrations, explaining systems and decisions.

### A2

- **Work and routine.** What do you do in your job? Describe a normal working day.
  - Follow-up: What do you do first in the morning?
  - Push: Which task takes most of your time, and why?
  - Listen for: present simple, time phrases
- **Past events.** What did you work on last week?
  - Follow-up: Who did you work with?
  - Push: Was it a typical week for you?
  - Listen for: past simple, regular and irregular verbs
- **Plans.** What are you going to work on this week?
  - Follow-up: And after that?
  - Push: What do you need from other people to finish it?
  - Listen for: going to, simple future forms

### B1

- **Solving a problem.** Tell me about a bug or technical problem you fixed recently. What happened and what did you do?
  - Follow-up: How did you find the cause?
  - Push: What would you do differently now?
  - Listen for: past simple and past continuous, sequencing words
- **Change over time.** How has your product or your team changed since you joined?
  - Follow-up: What is the biggest change?
  - Push: Is it better now? Why?
  - Listen for: present perfect, comparatives
- **Status update.** Give me a short status update on your current task, as you would in a daily meeting.
  - Follow-up: Is anything blocking you?
  - Push: When will it be ready, and what does that depend on?
  - Listen for: present continuous and present perfect, future forms, blockers

### B2

- **Technical decision.** Tell me about a technical decision your team made. What were the options, and why did you choose that one?
  - Follow-up: What was the main risk?
  - Push: What would the other option have given you?
  - Listen for: comparing, justifying, contrast
- **Hypothesis.** If you could change one thing in your team's development process, what would it be and why?
  - Follow-up: Who would disagree?
  - Push: What might go wrong?
  - Listen for: second conditional, cause and effect
- **Explaining to a non-expert.** Explain how a system you work on handles a request, to a colleague who is not technical.
  - Follow-up: Why does that step matter?
  - Push: What do people usually misunderstand about it?
  - Listen for: paraphrase, linking, clear structure

### C1

- **Trade-offs.** How should a team balance delivery speed and code quality when the two conflict?
  - Follow-up: Where is the limit for you?
  - Push: Argue the opposite view.
  - Listen for: nuance, concession, precise vocabulary
- **Incident review.** Think of a serious incident. How would you explain to senior managers what went wrong and what must change?
  - Follow-up: What would you leave out, and why?
  - Push: How do you talk about responsibility without blaming people?
  - Listen for: diplomatic language, complex sentences
- **Speculation.** How do you think engineering work will change in the next ten years?
  - Follow-up: What makes you think so?
  - Push: What would have to be true for you to be wrong?
  - Listen for: speculation, hedging, idiomatic range
